The regatta

Default order: longest unupdated first.

Why “the regatta”? Because Wikipedia and Wikidata can give brands a competitive advantage in some AI results, the regatta metaphor is useful and memorable. The wind is the market. It blows the same for every brand, more or less. Wikidata is the ballast: the weight of facts about the brand, normally just under the surface of AI responses. Wikipedia is the sail area (often cited in AI responses): what is written about the brand, with each statement backed by a published source. Ballast keeps the brand upright and accurately described; sail area moves it forward into view. Both are public, and any editor can change or challenge them.

The competitive view shows the set plus the highlighted brands, so you can compare them side by side.
Full category: every Wikidata item that is classified as a watch company (industry horology or watchmaking, product watch, or instance of watch manufacturing company) and has an English Wikipedia article, as returned by this query. Only brands that have customers, business or consumer, are included. Companies that Wikidata records as dissolved have no current customers and are left out.Competitors, per AI. What other brands should I consider?” I asked each question three times on each assistant: Claude (claude-opus-4-7), Gemini (gemini-2.5-pro), ChatGPT (gpt-6.1-sol). A brand is in this set if at least two of the three assistants named it. The set is refreshed monthly.

Column definitions
#
Rank by staleness within this category. 1 = longest without a substantive edit.
Brand
English Wikipedia article and Wikidata item (Q-id). Click either to see the source.
Last substantive edit
Most recent English Wikipedia edit of 50 bytes or more by a non-bot account that was not later reverted.
90d edits / editors
Edits to the English article in the 90 days to the issue date, and how many distinct accounts made them.
Reverted
Of those 90-day edits, how many were reverted. A measure of contestation.
Statements (referenced)
Number of statements on the Wikidata item, and how many carry at least one reference.
Ballast
Share of Wikidata statements that carry a reference. Sourced, complete Wikidata is what lets the record hold up.
Identity links out
Statements on the item that point to the brand's own products, brands, subsidiaries, holdings, founders, origin, motto, awards or executives. What the item says the brand is.
Key props
Of six properties a brand item should carry: industry, product, parent, subsidiaries, owner-of, Google Knowledge Graph id.
Editions
Language Wikipedias (and sister projects) with a page linked to this item. Sail area.
Refs
Reference tags in the English article.
Words
Approximate word count of the English article.
Reader views
English Wikipedia views in the stated month from Wikimedia's "user" agent class: traffic that presents as a browser. Not a guarantee of a human.
Spider
Views from declared crawlers such as Googlebot.
Automated
Views from undeclared traffic that Wikimedia's heuristics flag as bot-like. The scraper signal.
Which brands are included

Only brands that have customers, business or consumer, are included. Companies that Wikidata records as dissolved have no current customers and are left out.
