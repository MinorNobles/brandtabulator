BrandTabulator

When donors ask AI about a charity or about where to give, it can lean heavily on Wikipedia and Wikidata. BrandTabulator measures how fully and recently each organization is described on those sites, and how often it is read by people and machines. BrandTabulator refreshes every week, so bookmark this tab.

Of course, there are many sources that AI relies on. My work is to Diagnose the responses, Investigate the sources they credit, and Prescribe what can be done to improve the visibility and accuracy of a brand's descriptions in AI (DIP).

The regatta

Default order: longest unupdated first.

Why “the regatta”? Even if you have never been near a boat, you know the sails power it and a keel or ballast keeps it stable. The difference between Wikipedia and Wikidata is not obvious, hence the metaphor. Wikipedia is the sail area (often cited in AI responses): what is written about the brand, with each statement backed by a published source. Wikidata is the ballast: the weight of facts about the brand, normally just under the surface of AI responses and one of the sources behind Google’s Knowledge Graph. Ballast helps AI recognize the brand as a real thing and describe it accurately, while sails move it forward into view. The boat is your brand. The regatta is a competition for visibility in AI responses about your category.

Peers, per AI. On 9 November 2026, I asked three AI assistants, each searching the web, the questions a customer or marketer might ask: “What charities are similar to the Robin Hood Foundation?” “Which foundations fund anti-poverty work in New York City like the Robin Hood Foundation?” “I'm thinking about donating to the Robin Hood Foundation. What other charities should I consider?” I asked each question three times on each assistant: Claude (claude-opus-4-7), Gemini (gemini-2.5-pro), ChatGPT (gpt-6.1-sol). A brand is in this set if at least two of the three assistants named it. A brand counts as named only when two separate AI readings of the answer agree. The set is refreshed monthly. Also named by AI but not shown, because they have no English Wikipedia article of their own: Altman Foundation, Oxfam America, The Pinkerton Foundation, Tiger Foundation and The HOPE Program. Column definitions
#
Rank in this category. 1 = the brand whose Wikipedia article has gone longest without a real update.
Brand
Links to the brand's Wikipedia article and its Wikidata entry.
Last substantive edit
The last real change to the brand's English Wikipedia article: not a tiny fix, not a bot, and not undone later. Click the date to see it.
90d edits / editors
Changes to the English article in the last 90 days, and how many different people made them.
Reverted
How many of those changes were undone. A sign the article is being argued over.
Statements (referenced)
Facts recorded in the brand's Wikidata entry, and how many of them cite a source.
Ballast
Share of the brand's Wikidata facts that cite a source. Sourced facts are harder to dispute.
Identity links out
How many facts in the brand's Wikidata entry connect it to its own products, brands, companies it owns, founders, home country, slogan, awards or leaders. This is what Wikidata says the brand is.
Key props
How many of six basic facts the Wikidata entry has: industry, products, parent company, subsidiaries, companies owned, and Google's ID for the brand.
Editions
How many language versions of Wikipedia, and related sites, have a page on the brand.
Refs
Sources cited in the English article.
Words
Length of the English article, in words.
Reader views
Visits to the English article last month that looked like people using a browser. Some may still be machines.
Spider
Visits from search engines and other crawlers that identify themselves, such as Googlebot.
Automated
Visits from machines that don't identify themselves, which Wikimedia flags as automated. Often data scrapers.
Which brands are included

This page lists the Robin Hood Foundation and the organizations that at least two of three AI assistants named as its peers, if the organization has its own English Wikipedia article. Organizations that Wikidata records as dissolved are left out.

How this page is madeComputed from public Wikimedia data: Wikipedia revision history and pageviews, Wikidata items and statements. Which brands appear follows a stated rule, on this page.
Agent classesReader = Wikimedia's "user" class, traffic that presents as a browser. Spider = declared crawlers. Automated = undeclared traffic flagged as bot-like. None measure AI retrieval through dumps or Enterprise feeds.
