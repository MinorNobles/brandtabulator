What this measures

When people ask AI assistants about products, the answers lean on the public record. This site looks at two parts of that record for each brand, and compares brands within a category.

The keel: facts. The brand's entry in Wikidata, a free database of facts that search engines and other knowledge graphs reuse. How many of its facts carry a source, whether the basics are filled in, and whether it points to the brand's own products and companies.

The sails: descriptions. The brand's English Wikipedia article. How many references it has, how many languages it exists in, how often it is edited, and when someone last made a real change to it.

It also counts how often machines load the article. It does not rank brands as products, and it does not say which brands are best.

Which brands are included

Each category has two lists. The full category comes from a published query, so anyone can reproduce it. A competitive set is a smaller list chosen by a person, and the page says who chose it and why.

Definitions

Brand
English Wikipedia article and Wikidata item (Q-id). Click either to see the source.
Last substantive edit
Most recent English Wikipedia edit of 50 bytes or more by a non-bot account that was not later reverted.
90d edits / editors
Edits to the English article in the 90 days to the issue date, and how many distinct accounts made them.
Reverted
Of those 90-day edits, how many were reverted. A measure of contestation.
Statements (referenced)
Number of statements on the Wikidata item, and how many carry at least one reference.
Ballast
Share of Wikidata statements that carry a reference. Sourced, complete Wikidata is what lets the record hold up.
Identity links out
Statements on the item that point to the brand's own products, brands, subsidiaries, holdings, founders, origin, motto, awards or executives. What the item says the brand is.
Key props
Of six properties a brand item should carry: industry, product, parent, subsidiaries, owner-of, Google Knowledge Graph id.
Editions
Language Wikipedias (and sister projects) with a page linked to this item. Sail area.
Refs
Reference tags in the English article.
Words
Approximate word count of the English article.
Reader views
English Wikipedia views in the stated month from Wikimedia's "user" agent class: traffic that presents as a browser. Not a guarantee of a human.
Spider
Views from declared crawlers such as Googlebot.
Automated
Views from undeclared traffic that Wikimedia's heuristics flag as bot-like. The scraper signal.

Details behind the definitions

  • Bot edits are edits by an account whose name ends in "bot", or edits tagged as bot edits. They are left out of "last substantive edit".
  • Reverted edits are edits Wikipedia itself tags as reverted. They are left out of "last substantive edit".
  • Last substantive edit looks at the article's most recent 200 revisions. If none of them qualifies, the field is blank.
  • Statements counts every statement on the Wikidata item, including identifiers such as stock tickers and database IDs.
  • Key properties checked: industry (P452), product (P1056), parent (P749), subsidiary (P355), owner of (P1830), Google KG id (P2671).
  • Identity links counted: product or material produced (P1056), brand (P1716), subsidiary (P355), owner of (P1830), notable work (P800), country of origin (P495), motto (P1451), award received (P166), significant event (P793), founded by (P112), chief executive officer (P169).
  • Editions counts every Wikimedia site linked to the item, including Wikimedia Commons, not only language Wikipedias.
  • References counts <ref> tags in the article's source, so a reference used twice counts twice. Word counts are approximate.

Views and machine visits

Views come from Wikimedia's public pageview service, for English Wikipedia, for the previous full calendar month. Wikimedia sorts traffic into three classes. Reader ("user"): traffic that looks like a person using a browser. Some automated traffic that disguises itself ends up here. Spider: crawlers that identify themselves, such as Googlebot. Automated: traffic Wikimedia's own checks flag as automated.

These counts cannot see the bulk copies of Wikipedia that search and AI companies take directly, through downloads and Wikimedia's paid feed. No one outside those companies can see that. Machine visits here are a floor, the same floor for every brand.

Rankings

The default order is by staleness: the brand that has gone longest without a substantive edit comes first. Any column can be used to sort. There is no combined score yet.

How the numbers are checked

All figures are fetched from Wikimedia's public services by a published script, which runs every Monday on GitHub. Each request identifies the project, and requests are spaced out as Wikimedia asks. The first run was compared figure by figure with a separate hand pull, and they matched. Each brand's record in the JSON carries the revision ids it was computed from, so any figure can be traced to the exact version of the article or item.

Planned, not yet in place: an automatic week-on-week check that stops publication if numbers jump implausibly, and a check of any written summary against the edits it describes. Until then the author reviews each issue.

If your brand is listed

Anyone can correct facts in Wikidata, including people who work for the brand. Wikimedia's Terms of Use require anyone who is paid to edit, such as staff or an agency, to disclose that on their account. English Wikipedia also asks people with a conflict of interest not to edit the article directly, and to suggest changes on the article's talk page instead.

Related work

  • Recoin: a Wikidata tool that shows which properties an item is missing compared with similar items.
  • Wiki3DRank (Universidad de Murcia): a model that ranks topics by Wikidata statements, language editions and article length.
  • Dror Poleg's Wikipedia Power Ranking: a weekly ranking of people by edits to their Wikipedia biographies.
